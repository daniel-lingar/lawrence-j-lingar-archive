# The Lawrence J. Lingar Archive

> An evidence-based archive documenting the life, family, military service, death, recovery, and legacy of Pvt. Lawrence J. Lingar (1914–1943).

**Status:** Active research project  
**Maintainer:** Daniel Lingar  
**Subject:** Pvt. Lawrence J. Lingar  
**Army serial number:** 37105310  
**Unit:** 27th Infantry Regiment, 25th Infantry Division  
**Official status:** Killed in Action  
**Official date of death:** January 10, 1943  
**Place:** Guadalcanal, Solomon Islands

## Purpose

This repository preserves and investigates the life and death of my great-uncle, Private Lawrence J. Lingar, an Arkansas soldier killed during the Guadalcanal campaign.

The project is intended to reconstruct, as far as surviving evidence permits, Lawrence's life before the Army, his family and community, his military service, the operations surrounding his death, his burial and repatriation, his personal effects, and the unusual oral history that emerged decades afterward.

## The central rule

**A source is not the same thing as a conclusion.**

This archive deliberately keeps primary government records, family-history material, oral accounts, military context, working research, inference, hypothesis, and narrative interpretation distinct.

A family story remains oral history unless independent evidence corroborates it. A plausible explanation remains a hypothesis. An error or contradiction in an official document is preserved rather than silently corrected. Unknown means unknown.

## Evidence classifications

- **DOCUMENTED FACT** — directly supported by an identified primary or reliable documentary source.
- **HISTORICAL CONTEXT** — established information about Lawrence's unit, battlefield, community, or period that does not by itself establish his individual actions.
- **FAMILY HISTORY** — information preserved through family records, genealogy, photographs, correspondence, or family knowledge.
- **ORAL ACCOUNT** — testimony or recollection communicated verbally and preserved by the family.
- **INFERENCE** — a conclusion reasonably suggested by evidence but not directly stated by it.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — a possible explanation under investigation.
- **UNRESOLVED** — evidence is insufficient, conflicting, or incomplete.

See [EVIDENCE-METHOD.md](EVIDENCE-METHOD.md).

## The Alaska telephone account

According to the account preserved within the Lingar family, decades after Lawrence's death an elderly man contacted Lawrence's family. He reportedly said he was calling from, or had lived in, Alaska; was intoxicated and crying; claimed that he had killed Lawrence; connected the death with Lawrence lighting a cigarette at night; and contacted the family more than once.

The caller's identity has not been established.

The official military material currently available to this project records Lawrence as killed in action but does not by itself establish the cigarette account as the cause or circumstance of his death.

**The Alaska telephone account is therefore preserved as ORAL HISTORY, not established military fact.**

See [03-oral-history/alaska-calls/](03-oral-history/alaska-calls/).

## A critical distinction

Research has identified friendly-fire material in the broader operational history surrounding the 27th Infantry Regiment. That historical context does not, standing alone, prove that Lawrence was killed in such an incident.

Likewise, the later telephone account does not establish that a documented wartime friendly-fire event was the event that killed Lawrence.

These remain separate evidentiary lines unless evidence is found that legitimately connects them.

## Core documentary source: the IDPF

A central source is Lawrence J. Lingar's Individual Deceased Personnel File (IDPF). The original scan belongs in `01-primary-sources/idpf/`. Transcriptions, summaries and interpretations must remain distinguishable from the source scan. No transcription replaces the original document.

## Known contradictions

The project will explicitly track conflicts involving matters such as company assignment, dates associated with death and Army entry, burial chronology, names and name forms, geographic descriptions, and the timing of the later telephone calls.

See [CONTRADICTIONS.md](CONTRADICTIONS.md).

## Research questions

Major open questions include Lawrence's complete service chronology, his precise assignment and location on January 10, 1943, Company-level records, identification of casualties in reported friendly-fire events, the exact date and recipient history of the Alaska calls, the caller's identity and service history, possible contemporary corroboration of the cigarette account, and whether Lawrence's letters, notebook, New Testament, pocket book or paybook survive.

See [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

## Source control

Every significant source should receive a stable identifier in [SOURCE-REGISTER.md](SOURCE-REGISTER.md). Original documents should never be overwritten by cleaned, restored, annotated, compressed, colorized, or reformatted derivatives.

## Historical integrity

This project does not require every mystery to have an answer.

If the evidence establishes something, it will be stated as documented. If it suggests something, it will be identified as inference. If the family remembers something, it will be preserved as family or oral history. If records conflict, the conflict will be documented. If something remains unknown, it will remain unknown.

**The purpose of the archive is not to force the evidence to fit the story. It is to preserve Lawrence's story while allowing the evidence to speak for itself.**

## About Lawrence

Private Lawrence J. Lingar was an Arkansas soldier who served with the 27th Infantry Regiment, 25th Infantry Division during World War II. He was officially recorded as killed in action on Guadalcanal on January 10, 1943, at twenty-eight years old.

But "Killed in Action" is a status, not a biography.

This project exists to recover everything that can still responsibly be known about the man behind those words.
