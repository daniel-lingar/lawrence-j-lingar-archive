This allows another researcher to inspect the same evidence and determine whether the conclusion is justified.

# Confidence

When useful, research conclusions may be described using qualitative confidence terms.

### HIGH CONFIDENCE

Supported by strong, direct, preferably independent evidence with no significant unresolved contradiction.

### MODERATE CONFIDENCE

Supported by meaningful evidence but containing an evidentiary gap, ambiguity, or unresolved issue.

### LOW CONFIDENCE

Possible and supported by some evidence, but substantial uncertainty remains.

### SPECULATIVE

A possibility worth investigating but not currently supported strongly enough to call a conclusion.

Confidence ratings apply to **research conclusions**, not to the authenticity of an original source unless specifically stated.

# Source Register

Every significant source should receive a stable Source ID in:

`SOURCE-REGISTER.md`

Source IDs should not be reused.

If a source is later reclassified or determined to contain an error, preserve the original Source ID and record the change.

Historical auditability is more important than making the archive appear perfectly clean.

# Research Notes

Research notes belong in:

`05-working-research/`

# Narrative Writing

Narrative material belongs separately from the evidence base.

This includes:

- "A Letter Across Eighty Years";
- essays;
- memorial writing;
- scripts;
- posters;
- audio narratives;
- and publication drafts.

Narrative writing may be emotionally direct.

The evidence register must remain technically precise.

A narrative may say what an event means to the family.

It should not manufacture certainty about what historically occurred.

# Corrections

When research changes a previous conclusion:

1. do not alter the underlying historical source;
2. update the research conclusion;
3. record why it changed;
4. identify the evidence responsible for the change;
5. preserve important superseded interpretations when useful for the research history.

The archive should show how knowledge developed.

---

# Standard for Publication

Before a factual claim moves into a finished documentary history or publication, ask:

- What source supports this?
- Is it primary, secondary, oral, or inferential?
- Is the source independent?
- Is there contradictory evidence?
- Am I stating more than the source actually establishes?
- Would another researcher be able to trace this statement back to its evidence?

If the answer to the final question is no, the citation chain is not finished.

# The Standard for Lawrence's Story

The archive has two obligations.

The first is to preserve the family's history.

The second is to distinguish what can be demonstrated from what remains uncertain.

Neither requires dismissing the other.

The official record may be incomplete.

The family account may preserve something important.

The family account may also contain details altered by decades of retelling.

A government document may contain an error.

A later researcher may discover evidence that changes the current reconstruction.

All of those possibilities can exist simultaneously.

The archive therefore follows one overriding rule:

> **Preserve the evidence. Preserve the provenance. Preserve the uncertainty.**

If the evidence eventually establishes what happened to Lawrence, the conclusion should emerge from the records.

If it does not, the archive should say so.

That is not a failure of the investigation.

That is historical integrity.
